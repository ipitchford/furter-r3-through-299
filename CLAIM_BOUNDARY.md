# Claim boundary

## Proved

For every integer `d` with `2 <= d <= 300`,

```text
radical((g[d,3], g[d+1,3], g[d+2,3]) Q[x1,x2,x3]) = (x1,x2,x3).
```

Equivalently, Furter's `R(3,n)` holds for every `1 <= n <= 299`.

The exact finite theorem implies Furter's corresponding length-two closure
equalities for `(4,k)` and `(k,4)`, `2 <= k <= 300`. The full `e=3`
Polydegree column was already released separately by Evidence Press through a
different all-degree argument, so that is not the novelty claim here.

## Also proved

For every prime `p >= 7`, put `K=floor((p+2)/4)`. Then `g[n,3]` is the zero
polynomial modulo `p` whenever `n=-k mod p` and `2 <= k <= K`. Consequently
no fixed prime can certify every degree through the GOOD-special-fibre route.

## Not proved

- Universal Furter `R(3)`.
- The unrestricted Strong Factorial Conjecture.
- The general Polydegree Conjecture.
- The two canonical Euler--socle identities in all degrees.
- The existence of a finite-prime GOOD product automaton.

## Assurance

The finite theorem has exact receipts, negative controls, ordinary/optimized
parity, a frozen manifest, and a fresh-extraction replay. It is not formally
verified, independently reconstructed outside the producing workflow, or
specialist peer reviewed.
